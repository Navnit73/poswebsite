Managing inventory in a neighborhood medical store or chemist shop is a delicate balancing act. Stock too little, and you turn away sick patients looking for essential medications. Stock too much, and your limited working capital gets locked up in slow-moving syrups, expensive specialty injectables, and products that risk expiring on the shelf.

Whether you run a standalone retail medical shop or a small-chain dispensary, these **7 proven inventory management strategies** will help you streamline stock control, prevent stockouts, and protect your profit margins.

---

## 1. Categorize Stock Using ABC & VED Analysis

Not all medicines demand the same level of attention. Combine **ABC Analysis** (based on consumption value) with **VED Analysis** (based on clinical criticality):

- **Vital & High-Value (Category A-Vital)**: Life-saving drugs, insulin, cardiac medications. Maintain strict safety buffers and daily counts.
- **Essential & Moderate-Value (Category B-Essential)**: Broad-spectrum antibiotics, pain relievers, hypertensive pills. Review weekly with dynamic reorder points.
- **Desirable & Low-Value (Category C-Desirable)**: Bandages, vitamin supplements, throat lozenges. Order in bulk batches to capture wholesaler discounts.

---

## 2. Replace Static Min/Max with Dynamic Reorder Points (ROP)

Static minimum/maximum quantities fail when seasonal diseases spike or supplier lead times fluctuate. Use the **Dynamic Reorder Point Formula**:

$$\text{Reorder Point (ROP)} = (\text{Average Daily Usage} \times \text{Supplier Lead Time}) + \text{Safety Stock}$$

*Example*: If your pharmacy sells 15 strips of Cetirizine 10mg per day, the distributor takes 2 days to deliver, and you keep a 3-day safety buffer (45 strips):
$$\text{ROP} = (15 \times 2) + 45 = 75 \text{ strips}$$
The moment your on-hand stock drops to 75 strips, your software generates a purchase order.

---

## 3. Enforce Strict Batch & Expiry Tracking at Inward Receiving

The biggest mistake retail chemists make is dumping new stock onto shelves without logging lot numbers and expiry dates into the computer. 

Always:
- Scan the 2D DataMatrix barcode during unpacking.
- Verify invoice purchase rates, tax rates, and free goods schemes against the physical delivery challan.
- Flag any stock received with less than 6 months remaining shelf life for immediate vendor clarification.

---

## 4. Implement FEFO (First-Expiry, First-Out) at the Counter

Ensure counter staff always dispense the earliest-expiring batch first. Automated pharmacy point-of-sale software surfaces the nearest-expiry lot directly on the checkout screen, preventing older medication boxes from languishing behind new stock.

---

## 5. Conduct Continuous Cycle Counting Instead of Annual Physical Audits

Shutting down your medical store for a stressful weekend of annual stock counting is outdated and disruptive. Instead, practice **rolling cycle counts**:

- Count 10 high-value SKUs every morning before opening.
- Audit one specific rack or pharmaceutical schedule each week.
- Reconcile stock movement variances immediately while invoices and returns are fresh in memory.

---

## 6. Track Fast-Moving vs. Dead Stock Monthly

Run monthly stock velocity reports to identify:
- **Fast Movers**: Products turning over more than 8 times per year — negotiate volume rebates with distributors.
- **Slow Movers**: Products untouched for 60+ days — reduce reorder quantities.
- **Dead Stock**: Products with zero sales in 90+ days — arrange wholesaler return credits or promotional bundle clearance.
